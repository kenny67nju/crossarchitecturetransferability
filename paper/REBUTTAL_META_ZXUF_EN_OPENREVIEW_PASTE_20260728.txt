We thank the Area Chair for identifying the issues most important to the decision. We audited the implementation and cached experimental outputs and address each point below. We also narrow several claims where the submission was too broad.

1. Exact alignment definition and implemented score

The phrase "fixed reference encoder" was imprecise. We fix a reference DATASET R (STL-10 in the main experiments), not one encoder shared across candidates. Each candidate encoder m separately produces target features H(m,T) and reference features H(m,R). Both therefore lie in that candidate's own feature space and have the same feature dimension. Different candidates may have different dimensions, but their subspaces are never compared with one another; only their final scalar scores are compared.

Let the truncated SVDs of H(m,T) and H(m,R) provide top right-singular bases V(T) and V(R), using k(m)=min(k,n(T),n(R),d(m)) and k=20 by default. The implemented alignment is

Formula: $\\mathrm{SA}(k;H(m,T),H(m,R))=\\frac{\\cos^{2}(\\theta(1))+\\cdots+\\cos^{2}(\\theta(k(m)))}{k(m)}.$

Here the theta values are the principal angles obtained from the singular values of transpose(V(T))V(R). Thus no common cross-encoder projection is needed.

RankMe effective rank uses singular-value normalization:

Formula: $p_i=\\frac{\\sigma_i}{\\sum_j\\sigma_j},\\qquad r_{\\mathrm{eff}}(H)=\\exp\\Big(-\\sum_i p_i\\log p_i\\Big).$

For display clarity, let effrank(H) denote this effective rank and H(ref) the candidate's reference-feature matrix. The score used for every reported SSR result is

Formula: $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}(H,T)=\\frac{\\log\\operatorname{effrank}(H)-\\log\\mathrm{SA}(k;H,H(\\mathrm{ref}))}{\\log C(T)}.$

The general family is

Formula: $\\mathrm{SCR}(H,T;\\beta,g)=\\frac{\\log\\operatorname{effrank}(H)+\\beta g(\\mathrm{SA}(k;H,H(\\mathrm{ref})))}{\\log C(T)}.$

Setting $g(s)=-\\log s$ and $\\beta=1$ gives SSR exactly. The submitted equation that did not yield this expression by substitution was a presentation error. Alignment is clipped below at $10^{-12}$ before taking its logarithm. C(T) is task metadata (class count); it uses no sample labels and is common to all candidates for a fixed task.

2. Reference selection and practical scope

STL-10 is a practical rather than theoretically optimal default: it is small, diverse, processable by every candidate, and disjoint from the seven non-STL VTAB targets. We recommend a reference that is target-disjoint, broadly covered by the candidate encoders, sufficiently diverse, and large enough to estimate the top-k subspace. It should be selected without target labels or downstream validation.

Our corrected audit gives VTAB regret 0.053 with STL-10. A target-disjoint leave-one-target-out median over STL-10, CIFAR-10, Flowers-102, and Pets gives 0.051; when a target occurs in this set, it is removed before aggregation. A centered-SVD sensitivity run gives 0.044 and does not change the conclusion. The submitted Flowers-102 single-reference value 0.013 used the evaluated target as its reference and is withdrawn. The submitted reference-ablation values also used the legacy squared-singular-value normalization rather than RankMe's normalization.

Because the ensemble improves 0.053 only to 0.051 while requiring reference feature extraction and alignment once per candidate per reference, we do not recommend it as the default. A single target-disjoint generic reference is the cost-effective default; an ensemble is an optional robustness check when no clearly suitable reference exists.

The operational rule is: use SSR across heterogeneous architecture families, use RankMe within one family, and in a mixed zoo use SSR for family selection followed by RankMe for within-family variant selection. SSR is not a universal replacement for RankMe.

3. Corrected theoretical claim

We agree that the submitted Marchenko--Pastur derivation is incorrect as written. It mixed squared-singular-value normalization with RankMe's singular-value normalization, omitted the required size-biased spectral term, and incorrectly stated that the MP support is always separated from zero.

Let the eigenvalues of the normalized Gram matrix be lambda values and define $m_{1/2}=\\int\\sqrt{x}\\,d\\mu_{\\rho}(x)$. RankMe has probabilities proportional to square-root(lambda). The corrected limiting entropy is

Formula: $H(p)=\\log d+\\log m_{1/2}-\\frac{1}{2m_{1/2}}\\int\\sqrt{x}\\log x\\,d\\mu_{\\rho}(x)+o(1).$

Using the convention square-root(0) log(0)=0, the integral remains finite when the support reaches zero or contains an atom at zero. Consequently, for fixed positive aspect ratio, effective-rank(H)=Theta(d) with a positive aspect-ratio-dependent constant. This supports the RankMe width-confounding result.

We withdraw the broad Corollary 2 claim for arbitrary rotation-invariant scores. Rotation invariance alone does not imply monotonicity in d. VNE and trace-form H-score require separate analyses. We also withdraw the wording that SSR "breaks rotation invariance": under a joint basis change of target and reference features, alignment is correctly invariant. The precise distinction is that RankMe depends only on the target singular spectrum, whereas SSR is a two-input relational score that also depends on target-reference geometry.

4. DiSCO and STL-10 protocol

Our protocol audit confirms that official classification DiSCO uses target GROUND-TRUTH labels to construct class-conditional quantities. It does not use pseudo-labels or unlabeled clustering in our corrected evaluation. DiSCO is therefore a label-dependent comparator, not a same-budget label-free baseline.

We reimplemented the official method and completed the missing linear-probe cells. Corrected DiSCO regrets for group counts G=6/G=8 are: MV 0.146/0.146, VTAB 0.061/0.044, SSL 0.077/0.071, and EX 0.045/0.049. G is the number of consecutive near-equal spectral groups after PCA-128. These results invalidate the submitted DiSCO aggregate and its significance comparison. SSR remains stronger on MV (0.098 versus 0.146), whereas DiSCO is stronger on EX (0.045--0.049 versus 0.060) and competitive on VTAB and SSL. We withdraw any claim that SSR generally outperforms DiSCO.

STL-10 cannot simultaneously serve as an independent target and the reference for SSR. We therefore exclude it uniformly from the same-budget aggregate. On the resulting 22 pool-task units, corrected RankMe-normalized results are SSR regret 0.066, RankMe regret 0.169, and paired difference -0.1025. A target-name-clustered bootstrap over 10 unique targets gives 95% CI [-0.164,-0.039] and two-sided p=0.00114; paired Wilcoxon gives p=0.00254. The submission's 23-unit accounting and old values are superseded by this common-support result.

5. Evidence and limitations

There is no formal bound connecting subspace alignment to downstream linear-probe accuracy, and we will state this explicitly. The evidence is mechanistic and empirical: on 126 VTAB architecture-task pairs, alignment correlates at -0.964 with the fraction of between-class variance outside the generic reference subspace, which in turn positively predicts linear-probe accuracy. This motivates the correction but does not guarantee success for every pool.

Accordingly, our defensible claim is narrower: SSR significantly improves over the same-budget label-free RankMe baseline on heterogeneous common support. It does not uniformly outperform label-dependent DiSCO, and its weaker behavior on SSL and within-family pools is a real scope limitation.

6. Two-stage evidence

On the five-task MV multi-variant benchmark, flat SSR has regret 0.098 at mean cost 118.8 forward evaluations; SSR-to-RankMe has regret 0.102 at mean cost 38.0. This is evidence for feasibility on MV, not a general one-third-cost guarantee for arbitrary model zoos. We will moderate the abstract and conclusion accordingly and describe the result as a five-task proof of concept.

In summary, the implementation is now precisely specified and algebraically reconciled; the RankMe-specific MP consequence is corrected while the unsupported general corollary and rotation-breaking claim are withdrawn; the DiSCO and STL-10 protocols are corrected; and the empirical and cost-quality claims are narrowed to what the evidence supports.
