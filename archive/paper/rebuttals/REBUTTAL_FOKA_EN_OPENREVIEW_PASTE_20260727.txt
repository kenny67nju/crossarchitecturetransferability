1. Step-by-step algorithm and heterogeneous feature dimensions

The phrase "fixed reference encoder" is misleading, and our presentation likely caused it. To be precise: we fix a reference DATASET $R$ (STL-10 in the main experiments), not one encoder shared across candidates.

For each candidate encoder $m$, the same encoder processes target and reference images, producing features $H_{m,T}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{n_T\\times d_m}$ and $H_{m,R}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{n_R\\times d_m}$. The selection scores audited below use direct (uncentered) SVD, matching the submitted main scoring pipeline.

Therefore, within each alignment computation, the two feature dimensions are identical. Different candidates may have different $d_m$, but their right-singular subspaces are never compared with one another. No common feature space or cross-encoder projection is needed.

Let $H_{m,T}=U_T\\Sigma_T V_T^\\top$ and $H_{m,R}=U_R\\Sigma_R V_R^\\top$ be the SVDs, and let $k_m=\\min(k,n_T,n_R,d_m)$, with $k=20$ in the reported experiments. The implemented alignment is

For display clarity, let H(m,T), H(m,R), and k(m) denote the target features, reference features, and retained rank for candidate m. Then

Formula: $\\mathrm{SA}(k;H(m,T),H(m,R))=\\frac{\\cos^{2}(\\theta(1))+\\cdots+\\cos^{2}(\\theta(k(m)))}{k(m)}.$

where $\\theta_i$ are the principal angles between the top right-singular subspaces in candidate $m$'s own $d_m$-dimensional representation space.

A compact algorithm for candidate $m$, target $T$, reference $R$, and subspace size $k$ is:

- Step 1: $H_T\\leftarrow$ features of $T$ extracted by $m$;
- Step 2: $H_{\\mathrm{ref}}\\leftarrow$ features of $R$ extracted by $m$;
- Step 3: $V_T\\leftarrow$ top-$k_m$ right-singular vectors of $H_T$;
- Step 4: $V_{\\mathrm{ref}}\\leftarrow$ top-$k_m$ right-singular vectors of $H_{\\mathrm{ref}}$;
- Step 5: $\\mathrm{SA}\\leftarrow\\|V_T^\\top V_{\\mathrm{ref}}\\|_F^2/k_m$;
- Step 6: $p_i\\leftarrow\\sigma_i(H_T)/\\sum_j\\sigma_j(H_T)$;
- Step 7: $r_{\\mathrm{eff}}\\leftarrow\\exp(-\\sum_i p_i\\log p_i)$;
- Step 8: return $\\mathrm{score}\\leftarrow(\\log r_{\\mathrm{eff}}-\\log\\mathrm{SA})/\\log C(T)$.

Thus the effective rank and final experimental score are

Formula: $p_i=\\frac{\\sigma_i}{\\sum_j\\sigma_j},\\qquad r_{\\mathrm{eff}}(H)=\\exp\\Big(-\\sum_i p_i\\log p_i\\Big),$

Here effrank(H) is the effective rank and H(ref) is the reference-feature matrix.

Formula: $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}(H,T)=\\frac{\\log\\operatorname{effrank}(H)-\\log\\mathrm{SA}(k;H,H(\\mathrm{ref}))}{\\log C(T)}.$

Equivalently, the general family is

Formula: $\\mathrm{SCR}(H,T;\\beta,g)=\\frac{\\log\\operatorname{effrank}(H)+\\beta g(\\mathrm{SA}(k;H,H(\\mathrm{ref})))}{\\log C(T)}.$

Setting $g(s)=-\\log s$ and $\\beta=1$ gives the SSR expression exactly.

Alignment is clipped below at $10^{-12}$ before its logarithm. $C(T)$ is task metadata and only rescales all candidates for a fixed target, so omitting it leaves selection unchanged. Only the returned scalar is compared across candidates; heterogeneous dimensions require no additional projection.

2. Reference selection when STL-10 is unsuitable

STL-10 is a practical default, not a theoretically optimal reference. We selected it because it is small, contains diverse natural objects, can be processed by all candidates, and is disjoint from the seven non-STL VTAB target tasks.

When STL-10 is unavailable or inappropriate, we recommend the following ordered criteria:

- Target disjointness: never use the evaluated target itself as its reference.
- No target-performance selection: do not choose or tune the reference using target labels or target accuracy.
- Modality compatibility: use the same broad modality as the deployment domain, such as natural images, medical images, or remote-sensing images.
- Broad rather than narrow coverage: prefer diverse generic content over a highly task-specific dataset.
- Stable estimation: include enough diverse samples to estimate the top-$k$ subspace.
- Universal availability: every candidate must be able to process the reference with the same feature interface.

The reference may account for the broad nature of the downstream domain, but it must be chosen independently of downstream performance. Otherwise reference selection itself becomes a supervised model-selection procedure.

The submitted ablation values used the legacy squared-singular-value normalization; its Flowers-102 single-reference value $0.013$ also used the evaluated target as reference and is withdrawn. Recomputing with RankMe's stated normalization gives STL-10 regret $0.053$. A target-disjoint four-reference LOO median gives $0.051$: whenever the evaluated target is one of STL-10, CIFAR-10, Flowers-102, or Pets, it is removed before aggregation. Thus $0.051$ is not a self-reference result. A centered-SVD sensitivity run gives LOO regret $0.044$, leaving the conclusion unchanged.

3. Should an ensemble be the default, and what does it cost?

One reference requires one additional unlabeled reference-side feature extraction per candidate. These features are computed once, cached, and reused across every target. If there are $M$ references, the reference-side cost and storage increase approximately $M$-fold; the target-side cost is unchanged. Computing the median alignment over cached references is negligible compared with feature extraction.

The LOO ensemble changes VTAB regret only from $0.053$ to $0.051$, which does not justify making multiple references mandatory. Our recommendation is:

- default: one generic, target-disjoint reference;
- robustness-sensitive deployment: a small, predetermined ensemble, aggregated by the median;
- never include the evaluated target among its own references.

This preserves the lightweight character of the default method while providing a clear option when reference sensitivity matters.

4. Practical decision rule: $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}$ or RankMe?

We agree that the submission lacked an operational decision rule. $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}$ is not a universal replacement for RankMe.

- Across heterogeneous architecture families: use $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}$. This is the setting in which width varies and RankMe is confounded.
- Within a single architecture family: use RankMe. Dimensions and dominant subspaces are similar, and the alignment term does not provide a stable additional signal.
- Mixed model zoo with multiple variants per family: use $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}$ for family selection, followed by RankMe for within-family variant selection.

On the five-task MV multi-variant benchmark, flat $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}$ has regret $0.098$ at mean cost $118.8$, while $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}\\to$RankMe has regret $0.102$ at mean cost $38.0$. This supports the two-stage division of labor on MV, but we agree that five tasks constitute a proof of concept rather than a general cost-quality guarantee.

5. Status of the geometric premise

We agree that there is no formal bound connecting $\\mathrm{SA}_k$ to linear-probe accuracy. The evidence should be described as an empirical mechanism, not a theorem about downstream performance.

On 126 VTAB architecture-task pairs, $\\mathrm{SA}_k$ is strongly negatively correlated with the fraction of between-class variance lying outside the generic reference subspace ($\\rho=-0.964$). That architecture-specific variance fraction, in turn, positively predicts LP accuracy. This explains why subtracting alignment can be useful, but it does not guarantee success on every pool or within every family.

6. Updated scope relative to DiSCO

Our audit confirms that official DiSCO uses target GROUND-TRUTH labels to construct class-conditional quantities. It is therefore label-dependent, not label-free. We reimplemented the official PCA-128 procedure and completed all missing feature and LP cells. Here $G$ is the number of consecutive, near-equal spectral groups after PCA; VTAB excludes STL-10 and other pools use complete target sets. Corrected regrets ($G=6$ / $G=8$) are:

DiSCO is stronger than submitted, particularly on EX, so we withdraw any claim that SSR generally outperforms it. The same-label-budget comparison remains SSR versus RankMe. Excluding STL-10 and correcting the VTAB table's legacy squared-singular-value effective ranks, 22 pool-task units give regrets $0.066$ and $0.169$, and difference $-0.1025$. A target-name-clustered bootstrap over 10 unique targets gives 95% CI $[-0.164,-0.039]$ and $p=0.00114$; paired Wilcoxon gives $p=0.00254$.

In summary, the intended claim is narrower: $\\mathrm{SSR}^{\\ddagger}$ is a lightweight, parameter-free, label-free correction for cross-family width confounding. RankMe remains appropriate within a family; reference selection and the link to downstream accuracy remain empirical limitations; and the two-stage result should be interpreted only within the evaluated benchmark.
